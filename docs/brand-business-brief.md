# Myelix Community — Brand Brief

## overview

Myelix is a cognitive health monitoring platform. This brief defines the brand for the community product — the public, open-source, consumer-facing application. The brand must work for three distinct audiences simultaneously: health-aware individuals who want to monitor their own cognitive patterns, researchers who want a trustworthy data collection platform, and open-source contributors who want a technically serious project to work on.

The brand problem is that cognitive health monitoring sits in a minefield of bad precedents: wellness apps that oversell, surveillance tools masquerading as health tools, and clinical products that are inaccessible to ordinary people. Myelix must be none of those things. The brand must communicate that clearly.

## brand foundation

**purpose**
Give people a private, accurate, long-run window into their own cognitive patterns — without requiring data surrender, clinical infrastructure, or daily effort.

**belief**
Your behavioral patterns are yours. The signals your body and habits produce should generate insight for you, not data for someone else's business. A health monitor should be on your side. That means it stores your data on your device, not their servers.

**promise**
Myelix observes quietly, models accurately, and reports honestly. It does not alarm, speculate, diagnose, or share. It tells you how your patterns compare to your own baseline, and it does that entirely on your device.

**values**

- Privacy as architecture. Not a policy, not a promise, not a checkbox. The system is designed so that transmitting raw biometric data is technically impossible.
- Scientific rigor without clinical overreach. The model is grounded in behavioral science. The output is a pattern indicator, not a medical finding. The brand respects this distinction at all times.
- Quiet usefulness. The product earns trust by requiring nothing and delivering something. No daily rituals, no engagement mechanics, no social features. It works when you are not thinking about it.
- Openness. The code is public. The architecture is documented. The claims are auditable. This is not a marketing position — it is a design decision with brand implications.

## brand positioning

Myelix sits at the intersection of health technology, privacy technology, and open-source software. It does not compete with wellness apps on engagement metrics. It does not compete with clinical tools on medical credibility. It occupies a distinct space: the serious, private, long-run alternative to both.

The competitive differentiation is structural, not claimed. Any privacy-first claim can be verified by reading the source code. Any accuracy claim can be interrogated by reviewing the model architecture. The brand earns trust by making itself auditable, not by asserting trustworthiness.

**who Myelix is for**
Adults who have thought about cognitive health seriously. People who read about it, who have a parent with dementia and think about their own risk, who do quantified self work and want behavioral signal alongside physical signal. People who have downloaded other health apps and deleted them because the data practices were unacceptable. People who understand what "on-device inference" means and appreciate why it matters.

**who Myelix is not for**
People who want a score to gamify. People who want social comparison. People who need medical validation. People who are looking for an app that will tell them whether they are getting sick.

## visual identity direction

**general character**
Quiet. Precise. Scientific. The visual language should feel like a well-designed medical instrument, not a wellness app. Low contrast, generous whitespace, typographic hierarchy. No vibrant gradients, no playful iconography, no achievement badges.

**color**
A restrained palette. A deep neutral primary (near-black, not pure black — something with a slight warmth or cool blue cast). A single accent color that reads as precise and calm — consider a desaturated blue-green or slate. White space is a design element, not empty space to fill.

**typography**
Legible, serious, geometric. A sans-serif typeface with good numerical rendering (the resilience score is a number — it needs to read well at large display sizes). No display typefaces, no script. The type should feel like it belongs in a science journal, not a fitness app.

**iconography**
Minimal and functional. Icons are navigational aids, not decorative elements. No brain illustrations, no heart rate lines, nothing that evokes the consumer wellness aesthetic. If an icon is used, it should be so simple it disappears into the interface.

**motion**
Minimal and purposeful. Transitions should be quick and unobtrusive. No celebratory animations, no pulsing indicators. The only motion that belongs in this product is motion that communicates a state change clearly.

## product identity

**the name**
Myelix. The myelin sheath is the protective coating on nerve fibers that enables fast signal transmission — it degrades with age and stress, and its health is central to cognitive function. The name is scientific without being clinical, distinctive without being invented-word nonsense. It implies protection and signal fidelity.

**community edition**
The community edition is not a lite version or a free trial. It is the complete product for individual monitoring. "Community" signals open source and shared governance, not a downgrade. The name should be used when distinguishing from premium or institutional tiers, not as a default modifier on the product name. In most consumer contexts the product is simply "Myelix."

**voice**
The product and its communications speak in a calm, direct, specific voice. It does not motivate, encourage, or coach. It does not say "you're doing great" or "keep it up." It reports. The voice is that of a trusted instrument — accurate, consistent, without personality that would get in the way.

Written voice examples:
- "Your patterns have been consistent with your baseline for the past 7 days." (not: "Great week! Your brain is performing well.")
- "Collection was paused due to battery optimization settings." (not: "Oops! We couldn't track you today.")
- "Raw sensor data is stored locally and deleted after feature extraction. Nothing is transmitted." (not: "We take your privacy seriously and protect your data.")

**positioning in the open-source ecosystem**
In developer and researcher contexts, Myelix leads with the architecture. The brand in these contexts is about technical credibility: the code is clean, the documentation is complete, the design decisions are explained, the maintainers are responsive. Open-source credibility is earned, not marketed. The GitHub repository is the brand expression for this audience.
